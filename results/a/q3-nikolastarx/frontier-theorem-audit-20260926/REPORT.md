# P3 singleton frontier: a zero-spill lower-bound theorem

## Verdict and scope

**Yes, conditionally.** For a valid P3 submission with exactly one eligible original compute op per subgraph, a core's submitted `core_schedules` word fixes the order of those ops in the sequence supplied to official Step 2. The inclusive first-to-last interval sum of **original tensor IDs** touched by those ops, using the Task builder's local pool and byte size (`DDR` becomes `UB`), is a lower bound on that core's *counterfactual zero-spill* Step 2 allocation peak in each of `UB` and `L1`. If this frontier exceeds the pool capacity, Step 2 cannot finish with zero spills: it must insert a spill or fail. Under R9's stronger tensor-mediated-edge guard, this frontier is actually the **exact** no-spill peak of its Task graph. This is **not** a lower bound on the peak of a run *after* spills, and it does **not** prove official plan illegality. The 068/K5 static value 221,208 B > 131,072 B therefore rules out zero-spill Step 2 **for that exact singleton mapping and word, if its Task reaches Step 2**, but does not rule out a legal spilled plan. The existing 068 measurement is static; no official Task, Step, or evaluator was run here. [R9 frontier and plan, lines 364–398, 464–478](../../../../src/q3/layered_query_flow.py); [R9 port guard, lines 37–79](../../../../src/q3/attention_rows.py); [prior static diagnostic](../frontier-semantic-audit-20260926/REPORT.md); [problem brief](../layered-capacity-question-20260926/PRO_BRIEF.md).

## Precise statement and proof

Let `w_c=(u_1,...,u_m)` be a core's submitted singleton order. For each original tensor `q`, let `I_c(q)=[first_c(q),last_c(q)]` be the closed range of indices of its eligible producer or consumer ops on that core; omit tensors with no such incident op. Set `p(q)=UB` for original `DDR` and otherwise use its original `UB`/`L1` pool. Define `F_c,p(i)=sum(size(q): p(q)=p and i in I_c(q))`. Assume the official plan and graph pass input validation, the singleton ranks are all distinct, the Task construction follows the inspected frozen P3 code, and Step 2 receives its resulting complete topological `seq`. An overflow in Task construction or Step 1 is outside the conditional Step 2 claim.

The plan validator covers each eligible non-COPY op exactly once and places each subgraph exactly once in a core order. [plan validation, lines 122–175](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/stub_multicore_cut_and_schedule.py). The P3 Task builder copies every mapped op and, for each original tensor and each touched core, retains its original ID/size, changing only `DDR` placement to `UB`, and preserves its eligible producer/consumer incidence edges. [Task builder, lines 76–95, 141–164](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/multicore_cut_evaluate_problem_3.py). Generated input/output and cross-core COPYs add uses of these tensors; direct op-to-op cross-core edges can also create **new** local tensors. [Task builder, lines 166–242](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/multicore_cut_evaluate_problem_3.py).

Step 1 produces `raw_seq`; P3 then **stably** sorts it by submitted subgraph rank and checks that the result remains topological before calling Step 2. Since each rank has exactly one eligible original op, deleting generated COPYs from the resulting `seq` yields exactly `w_c`. The COPYs share the corresponding endpoint subgraph rank, and stable order within a rank does not reorder eligible ops. [priority sort, lines 51–67; Step 2 call, lines 244–259](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/multicore_cut_evaluate_problem_3.py). Thus an original tensor's first and last Task incidents bracket its first and last submitted eligible incidents.

In a run with **no spill**, Step 2 allocates a non-DDR tensor at its first incident op and retains it until after its last incident op. At each op it allocates all first uses, checks capacity, then frees last uses **after** the check. [uses and lifecycle, lines 39–67, 113–136; microsteps, lines 204–289](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py). Therefore, at the check for `u_i`, every original tensor whose submitted closed interval contains `i` is resident, including inputs at their last use and outputs at their first use. Extra COPY uses can only extend these lifetimes; they cannot remove a required original tensor without a spill. Hence `F_c,p(i) <= zero_spill_resid_c,p(u_i)` for both pools, and `max_i F_c,p(i) <= zero_spill_peak_c,p`. An `F > capacity` contradicts a successful zero-spill run.

This proof does **not** claim that every actual spilled run must have the original tensor resident through its entire interval: Step 2 can evict a non-current tensor, later generate a COPY_IN with a new physical incarnation, and rewire later consumers. [victim eligibility, lines 157–202; spill insertion, lines 291–455](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py). Spill allocation can reduce actual peak below the unspilled frontier. Capacity overflow triggers victim selection; only exhaustion of eligible victims raises the Step 2 error. [Step 2, lines 249–284](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py).

## Limits and a small strictness example

For **general** official graphs, the original-tensor frontier is necessary but not sufficient for zero spill, because the Task builder may add tensors absent from the original tensor list. Consider valid original graph ops `A -> B` by a direct op-to-op edge with `data_size=1`, placing `A` on core 0 and `B` on core 1; let a separate original 1-byte `UB` input tensor `y` feed `B`. Each op has its own singleton subgraph. On core 1 the original frontier at `B` is 1 byte (`y`), yet the Task builder creates an additional 1-byte local tensor for the direct cross-core edge and a COPY_IN for it. Both that tensor and `y` are current inputs at `B`, so Step 2's alloc-before-free check requires 2 bytes. With 1-byte UB capacity it cannot get a zero-spill pass (indeed no current-input victim is eligible). This is a source-level example, **not** an executed official case. [direct-edge conversion, lines 217–241](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/multicore_cut_evaluate_problem_3.py); [current-use exclusion, lines 157–173](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py).

R9's `_ports` guard rejects direct op-to-op edges and requires every original edge to be tensor-mediated; it also aligns its compute index with all eligible original ops. [R9 port guard, lines 37–79](../../../../src/q3/attention_rows.py). Consequently the Task graph has no new on-chip direct-edge tensor. Every generated COPY_IN/OUT touching an original tensor is assigned to the subgraph of its earliest consumer or latest producer, respectively, hence to a bucket within that tensor's submitted closed interval. [Task builder, lines 166–215](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/multicore_cut_evaluate_problem_3.py). At *every* Task step in bucket `i`, its original-tensor resident set is a subset of the frontier at `i`; at the singleton compute step, it contains the entire frontier. Therefore, for this guarded class, `max F` **equals** the exact hypothetical unspilled Task Step 2 peak, and `max F <= capacity` in both pools is necessary and sufficient for zero spill in Step 2, conditional on a valid accepted Task priority sequence. The R9 helper's `step2_no_spill_certificate` label is justified **within those guards**, but is not a certificate of Task/Step 3/whole-evaluator legality or P3 Makespan. [R9 helper, lines 364–398; plan construction, lines 464–478](../../../../src/q3/layered_query_flow.py).
