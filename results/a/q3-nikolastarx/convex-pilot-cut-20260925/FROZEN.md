## Single-set contraction lemma

For a DAG and vertex subset S, contracting **only S** produces a DAG iff no directed path with two endpoints in S has an internal vertex outside S. If such a path exists, contraction yields a directed cycle through its outside portion. Conversely, any quotient cycle must pass through the contracted vertex (the original DAG was acyclic); expanding the two incident quotient edges and the exterior cycle gives such an S-to-S path. The proposition says nothing about simultaneously contracting two sets.

For a contiguous interval of one core's singleton word, assume the base schedule is a valid topological extension of the original compute DAG (including indirect paths). Any same-core vertex on a path between interval members has an index between their indices and thus lies in the interval. Therefore a nonconvex path must leave that core. Dynamic propagation on a topological traversal stores, at each alien-core vertex, the maximum index of the most recent origin in this core; arrival at a core vertex j records the maximum reentry exit i and resets the propagated label to j. Direct same-core edges do not count as leaving. For a segment ending at r, take b=max reentry i among j<=r; its earliest safe start is L=b+1 (b=-1 gives 0). Reentries with i<L enter from outside, and every start <=b includes the witnessed pair. This is O(V+E) time and O(V+E) space given a topological order. Explicit full original op-arc quotient checks remain the final static check; singleton coverage and per-core schedule order are checked separately.
