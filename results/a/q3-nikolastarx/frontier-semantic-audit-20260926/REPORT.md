# P3 R9 static frontier versus official Step 2: bounded semantic audit

## Verdict

The 221,208 B UB peak in the 068/K5 diagnostic is **not a certificate of official illegality**. Official Step 2 first allocates all tensors used at an operation, tests capacity before last-use frees, and then repeatedly spills an eligible resident tensor until the pool fits. It errors only if capacity is still exceeded and no eligible victim exists (or the post-spill assertion fails). Thus even an **exact** no-spill peak above 131,072 B can lead to a legal plan with spills. Conversely, an exact peak at or below capacity means this Step 2 loop needs no spill for that fixed task graph and sequence; it says nothing by itself about later Step 3 or multicore acceptance. [Official `schedule_step2.py` lines 204–289](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py); [official `schedule_step3.py` lines 611–639](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step3.py).

For the **actual R9 static calculation**, even the implication “peak above capacity means official Step 2 must spill or fail” is **conditional**. The helper counts original tensors on each assigned compute core from first to last *compute-word bucket*, maps DDR positions to UB, and omits generated COPY operations and their placement; it does not build the official per-core Task graph or its Step 1/priority sequence. The supplied sources do not establish a one-to-one lifetime/byte correspondence between those buckets and official Step 2 uses. Accordingly the 068 number is a guard failure and a useful pressure diagnosis, not a necessary or sufficient test of official Step 2 legality or no-spill behavior. [R9 `layered_query_flow.py` lines 364–398, 451–479](../../../../src/q3/layered_query_flow.py); [diagnostic `diagnose.py` lines 34–88](../layered-fourtrack-frontier-diagnostic-20260926/diagnose.py); [diagnostic `RESULT.md` lines 3–9](../layered-fourtrack-frontier-diagnostic-20260926/RESULT.md).

## Exact correspondence needed for a no-spill claim

For a fixed **official Task graph and the exact `seq` passed to Step 2**, define a pool's live set at step `t` as every non-DDR tensor with at least one incident op in `seq`, whose first incident op is at or before `t` and last incident op is at or after `t`. Count inputs and outputs together at `t`: Step 2 allocates first uses before checking and only frees last uses afterward. If this set and the initial source-residency convention match the implementation, its maximum is the unspilled Step 2 alloc peak. A maximum `<= capacity` is necessary and sufficient for this Step 2 pass to insert **zero** spills; a maximum `> capacity` is necessary and sufficient for at least one overflow attempt, **not** for Step 2 failure. `tensor_uses` includes producer and consumer incident edges, while DDR tensors are excluded. [Official `schedule_step2.py` lines 39–67, 113–136, 204–289](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py).

The current static helper has the same inclusive first/last bucket convention, but on another object: it uses R9's `owner` and `words`, puts each original tensor in every touched compute core, substitutes `UB` for original `DDR`, and sums intervals without any spill or reincarnation. [R9 `layered_query_flow.py` lines 334–398](../../../../src/q3/layered_query_flow.py). Official Step 1 derives a topological order from the *task graph*; generated COPY_IN/COPY_OUT and Step 2 spill COPYs create additional physical uses, outputs, and incarnations. Step 2 anchors spill COPY_OUT after the victim's previous use and COPY_IN before its next use, then rewires consumers to new on-chip tensor IDs. These are allocation/transfer effects, not simply more static compute buckets. [Official `schedule_step1.py` lines 69–99, 104–198](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step1.py); [official `schedule_step2.py` lines 291–455](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py).

The distinction matters in both directions. A generated input COPY can begin a local lifetime before its first compute consumer; a generated output COPY can extend a source tensor's lifetime beyond its last local compute consumer. A different official `seq` can also change overlap among independent tensors. The static test neither models those events nor proves that Task generation preserves the same tensor identities, sizes, placement, and compute order. Therefore `static_peak <= capacity` is not an unconditional sufficient official no-spill certificate, and `static_peak > capacity` is not an unconditional proof that official Step 2 spills. The restricted file set did not include the Task builder or call-site sequence construction, so the actual 068/K5 correspondence remains unverified.

## Small Step 2 counterexample to “over capacity means illegal”

Take a one-pool capacity of 1 byte and a valid topological `seq = [A, B, C, D]`. `A` produces a 1-byte tensor `x` used later by `C`; independent `B` produces a 1-byte tensor `y` used later by `D`. Assume `C` and `D` add no on-chip output. The unspilled inclusive intervals are `x: A..C` and `y: B..D`, so the alloc peak at `B` is 2 > 1. At `B`, Step 2 can evict `x` because `x` is not used by `B` and has a future use. At `C`, it can evict `y`, reload `x`, and run `C`; then reload `y` for `D`. Each op fits after spilling. This is a direct trace of the Step 2 victim exclusion, future-use condition, and reload insertion, not an executed official Task/Step result. [Official `schedule_step2.py` lines 157–202, 212–289, 314–383](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step2.py). A hard capacity failure instead requires a step whose still-needed current tensors alone exceed capacity, or a state with no eligible future-use victim; the numerical no-spill peak alone does not distinguish these.

## Consequence for 068/K5 and remaining unknowns

The frozen diagnostic reports core 0 UB 221,208 B at local bucket 145 (op 4551), 81 counted live intervals and 90,136 B excess; it made zero official Task, Step, solver, or evaluator calls. The K4 comparison has the same nonempty core words and peaks. These facts establish that the present direct constructor rejects this candidate at its **own** guard (`step2_no_spill_certificate`), and that the empty fifth core did not create this particular static peak. They do not establish that official Step 2 would error, how many spills it would insert, or the P3 makespan/cache effect. [Diagnostic `RESULT.md` lines 3–9](../layered-fourtrack-frontier-diagnostic-20260926/RESULT.md); [R9 `layered_query_flow.py` lines 464–489](../../../../src/q3/layered_query_flow.py).

To resolve official semantics for this candidate, one would need the exact generated Task graph per core, the exact pre-Step-2 `seq`, its tensor-use/occupancy trace and `spill_records`/`overflow_log`, followed separately by Step 3 and whole-plan acceptance. Step 3 schedules physical incarnations, allocates outputs before issue, frees after all consumers, and can introduce memory-reuse dependencies whose acyclicity it checks; a Step 2 success alone does not certify the complete plan. This audit deliberately made no Task/Step/E0 call and did not inspect the Task builder. [Official `schedule_step3.py` lines 124–230, 239–295, 595–639](../../../../data/raw/a/official/code/schedule_step3.py).
