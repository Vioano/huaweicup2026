**Mechanism visible in this plan.** The official timeline is a repeated sequence of a short M/DDR Task wave followed by a compute-heavy Task wave. At 085, the three longest tasks in each compute wave finish together and gate the next wave; for example Tasks 12–14 run `15,093..91,734`, while Tasks 15–16 end at `66,914` and `54,781`, and Task 17 begins at `92,734`. The corresponding pattern repeats every ten Task IDs. On each of cores 0–1, Makespan exactly equals 764,703 occupied cycles plus nineteen 1,000-cycle inter-Task gaps. The eight 76,641-cycle waves plus the last 74,693-cycle wave alone occupy 687,821 cycles (87.8% of Makespan) on the limiting cores. Their current packet/core allocation, rather than a serial common-prefix Task, is the direct observed bottleneck. Case 005 has the analogous but smaller imbalance: Tasks 14–15 and 24–25 each run 9,426 cycles against 6,448 on the other three cores. Its shorter 42,487-cycle run is more exposed to seven inter-wave releases of roughly 1,000 cycles and to 1.34 MB of partition copies; these are observed costs, but their recoverable fraction cannot be read from the aggregate byte count. `result.json.gz:task_dependencies`, `per_core_timeline`, `data_movement_bytes`; `plan.json:core_schedules` support these statements. The candidate uses whole sink-exclusive packets and per-wave pipe-load packing (`src/q1/heavy_suffix.py:81-127`, `src/q1/sink_peel.py:24-75`), which explains why more cores can still receive shorter packets. The official gate is 1,000 cycles across cores (`docs/a/Q1_SINK_PEEL.md:38-44`).

**One falsifiable structural move.** Try a legality-checked *within-wave re-cut of an oversized sink-exclusive packet* when the predicted limiting-core load gap is large enough to pay for new cross-Task copies and waits. Preserve the existing number/order of waves and the common prefix. Use the actual contracted DAG and tensor edges, not a case-ID trigger, and reject a cut if quotient/core Task order cycles. First static check: show on 085 that a proposed re-cut transfers substantial compute work from one of the three 76,641-cycle Tasks to a lighter core while bounding newly cut tensor bytes; then apply the same rule to 005 and one distinct shape. Only after freezing the rule and a specific budget should an owner compare final E0 Makespan, added DDR, legality, and complete solver wall time against v4. A cut that merely adds gates or copies without shortening the repeated limiting waves refutes this move. The saved timeline does **not** prove such a legal and profitable cut exists.

## Research decision

Do not assume the necessary lower bound is attainable or that splitting large packets helps. A useful next construction must demonstrate a precedence-valid way to distribute work from the repeated limiting wave without merely adding a new global synchronization wave. Splitting every packet into two synchronized waves could preserve the same load imbalance while adding gates. A preemptive identical-machine wrap-around schedule is only an analogy: these packets are multi-Pipe DAGs, operations are indivisible, and all incoming Task dependencies delay the entire Task. Naively merging early independent work with a late dependent segment can serialize the Task quotient or even create a cycle.

The mathematical next step is a small input-driven decomposition with an explicit augmented-DAG rank/certificate, boundary-tensor accounting and a refusal condition. It should first be checked statically on the saved 085 graph; no new compiler/evaluator calls are authorized by this note. The current two-cell fusion result remains a separate established mechanism.
